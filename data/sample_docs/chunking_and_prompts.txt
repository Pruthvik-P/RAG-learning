Chunking Strategies
-------------------

Chunking is the act of splitting long documents into smaller passages before
embedding. Good chunks are self-contained: a reader (or a model) can understand
them without seeing the rest of the document.

Common strategies:

1. Fixed-size chunking: split every N characters with some overlap. Simple and
   predictable, but it can cut sentences in half.

2. Recursive chunking: try to split on paragraph breaks first, then line
   breaks, then sentences, then words. This preserves natural boundaries and is
   the most widely used default.

3. Sentence chunking: group complete sentences until a size budget is reached.
   Chunks stay grammatical, which tends to produce cleaner embeddings.

4. Semantic chunking: use embeddings to detect where the topic changes and cut
   there. Highest quality, but slower and more complex.

The overlap parameter controls how many characters neighbouring chunks share.
Overlap helps when an important idea straddles a boundary: it will appear whole
in at least one chunk. Typical overlap is 10 to 20 percent of the chunk size.

Prompt Construction
-------------------

The prompt is where retrieval becomes generation. A typical RAG prompt has four
parts:

- A system instruction telling the model to answer only from the context and to
  admit when it does not know.
- The retrieved context, often numbered [1], [2], [3] so the answer can cite
  sources.
- The user's question.
- A final instruction about the desired format of the answer.

Grounding rules reduce hallucination: instruct the model to quote or cite the
context, and to say "I don't know" when the answer is absent. Keeping the
temperature low (around 0.0 to 0.3) makes answers more focused and factual.

Evaluation
----------

Measure retrieval separately from generation. Retrieval metrics include hit
rate, mean reciprocal rank, and context precision. Generation metrics include
faithfulness (is every claim supported by the context?) and answer relevance.
Tracking both tells you whether to improve your search or your prompt.
